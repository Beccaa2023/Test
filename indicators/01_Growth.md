# Growth Handbook

## Purpose

The Growth Handbook explains how MOS measures the expansion and contraction of the real economy.

Economic growth is the starting point of every macro analysis.

Without understanding growth, it is impossible to correctly interpret inflation, monetary policy, corporate earnings or asset prices.

MOS therefore evaluates growth before all other macro variables.

---

# The Growth Framework

MOS divides economic growth into four layers.

Real Economy

↓

Business Activity

↓

Household Demand

↓

Production

↓

Income

↓

GDP

GDP is the final summary rather than the starting point.

MOS focuses on the drivers of GDP before GDP itself.

# Core Indicator 1

## GDP

Importance

★★★★★

Frequency

Quarterly

Publisher

Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)

Classification

Lagging

---

### What is GDP?

Gross Domestic Product measures the total value of all final goods and services produced within the United States.

GDP summarizes the economy.

It does not predict it.

---

### GDP Composition

Consumption (Personal Consumption Expenditures)

≈ 68–70%

Private Investment

≈ 17–20%

Government Spending

≈ 16–18%

Net Exports

Usually negative.

MOS always examines which component is driving GDP.

The same GDP growth rate can imply very different macro environments depending on its composition.

---

### Market Interpretation

GDP above expectations

Bullish for growth.

Potentially bearish for long-duration bonds if inflation pressure increases.

GDP below expectations

Suggests slowing activity.

May support Treasury bonds if inflation expectations decline.

---

### Limitations

Quarterly frequency.

Large publication lag.

Multiple revisions.

Cannot identify turning points early.

MOS never uses GDP as the first signal of economic change.

# Core Indicator 2

## GDPNow

Importance

★★★★★

Publisher

Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta

Frequency

Continuous

Classification

Coincident / Early Estimate

---

### Why GDPNow Matters

GDPNow estimates current-quarter GDP before official release.

It incorporates high-frequency data such as:

Retail Sales

Industrial Production

Trade

Housing

Inventories

GDPNow provides a timely estimate but is not an official forecast.

MOS uses GDPNow to monitor momentum between GDP releases.

---

### Common Mistakes

Do not treat GDPNow as a prediction.

It is a nowcasting model.

Large revisions are common as new data become available.

# Core Indicator 3

## ISM Manufacturing PMI

Importance

★★★★★

Frequency

Monthly

Publisher

Institute for Supply Management

Classification

Leading

---

### Why It Matters

Manufacturing responds quickly to changes in demand.

PMI therefore provides an early signal of changes in economic activity.

---

### Key Threshold

PMI > 50

Expansion.

PMI < 50

Contraction.

More important than the level is the direction of change.

A PMI rising from 46 to 49 often signals improvement even though it remains below 50.

---

### Subcomponents

MOS pays special attention to:

New Orders

Production

Employment

Supplier Deliveries

Inventories

Among these,

New Orders is generally the most forward-looking.

# Core Indicator 4

## Conference Board Leading Economic Index (LEI)

Importance

★★★★★

Frequency

Monthly

Classification

Leading

---

### Purpose

The LEI combines multiple forward-looking indicators into a composite index.

Persistent declines often precede economic slowdowns.

MOS treats the LEI as a cross-check rather than a standalone forecast.

---

### Why It Matters

Consumer spending accounts for roughly 70% of U.S. GDP.

Retail Sales therefore provide an important measure of household demand.

MOS compares headline Retail Sales with the Control Group measure, which better reflects underlying consumption used in GDP calculations.

# Common Analytical Mistakes

Mistake 1

Treating GDP as a leading indicator.

Correction:

GDP confirms.

PMI leads.

---

Mistake 2

Focusing only on headline Retail Sales.

Correction:

Review Control Group and revisions.

---

Mistake 3

Interpreting one month's data as a trend.

Correction:

Use three-month and six-month averages.

---

Mistake 4

Ignoring revisions.

Correction:

Always compare initial releases with revised figures.

# Key Questions

Every Growth analysis should answer:

Is growth accelerating or decelerating?

Which sectors are driving growth?

Are leading indicators confirming the trend?

Has the market already priced this information?

What are the implications for monetary policy and asset allocation?

---

# Final Principle

Growth is not a single number.

It is a dynamic process observed through multiple indicators.

MOS seeks to identify changes in growth before they appear in GDP.
