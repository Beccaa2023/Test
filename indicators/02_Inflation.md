# Inflation Handbook

## Purpose

The Inflation Handbook explains how MOS measures inflation, interprets inflation dynamics and evaluates the implications for monetary policy and financial markets.

Inflation is the bridge between the real economy and central bank policy.

Understanding inflation is essential for interpreting interest rates, bond yields, valuation and asset allocation.

MOS evaluates inflation as a process rather than a single statistic.

Importance

Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)

Frequency

Monthly

Classification

Coincident

---

### What is CPI?

The Consumer Price Index measures the average price change of a basket of goods and services purchased by urban consumers.

Headline CPI includes food and energy.

Core CPI excludes food and energy.

MOS analyses both.

---

### Why CPI Matters

CPI is the most widely followed inflation indicator.

Although the Federal Reserve officially targets PCE inflation, CPI strongly influences market expectations because it is released earlier.

Markets often react more to CPI than to PCE.

---

### Market Interpretation

Higher-than-expected CPI

↓

Inflation pressure

↓

Higher Treasury yields

↓

Higher probability of Fed tightening

↓

Negative for long-duration assets

Lower-than-expected CPI

↓

Lower inflation pressure

↓

Lower yields

↓

Positive for bonds and growth equities

---

### Common Mistakes

Do not focus only on the headline number.

Analyse:

Monthly change (MoM)

Year-over-year change (YoY)

Three-month annualized inflation

Component breakdown

Revisions

### Why Core CPI?

Food and energy prices are volatile.

Core CPI better reflects persistent inflation.

MOS treats Core CPI as a better measure of underlying inflation momentum.

# Core Indicator 3

## Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE)

Importance

★★★★★

Publisher

Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)

Frequency

Monthly

Classification

Coincident

---

### Why PCE Matters

The Federal Reserve officially targets PCE inflation.

PCE differs from CPI because it:

Uses a broader basket

Adjusts for consumer substitution

Includes medical expenditures paid by employers and government

Receives regular revisions

MOS therefore treats PCE as the most policy-relevant inflation measure.

# Core Indicator 5

## Producer Price Index (PPI)

Importance

★★★★☆

Publisher

BLS

Classification

Leading

---

PPI measures prices received by producers.

Producer prices often change before consumer prices.

Persistent increases in PPI may eventually feed into CPI and PCE.

MOS treats PPI as an early warning signal rather than a direct policy guide.

---

# Core Indicator 6

## ISM Prices Paid

Importance

★★★★☆

Classification

Leading

---

ISM Prices Paid reflects input cost pressures reported by purchasing managers.

Because firms observe changes in costs before official inflation statistics are published, this survey often provides an early indication of inflation trends.

# Inflation Expectations

MOS monitors both market-based and survey-based expectations.

Market-Based

• 5-Year Breakeven Inflation
• 10-Year Breakeven Inflation
• 5Y5Y Forward Inflation

Survey-Based

• University of Michigan Inflation Expectations
• New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations

Inflation expectations influence wage negotiations, pricing decisions and long-term interest rates.

Anchored expectations support price stability.

Unanchored expectations increase inflation risk.

---

# Shelter Inflation

Shelter represents the largest weight in CPI.

MOS monitors:

Owners' Equivalent Rent (OER)

Rent of Primary Residence

New Lease Indices

Because official shelter inflation responds slowly to market rents, MOS compares government data with private rental indicators.

Shelter inflation is one of the most lagging components of CPI.

---

# Wage Inflation

Labor costs are a major source of persistent inflation.

MOS monitors:

Average Hourly Earnings

Employment Cost Index

Unit Labor Cost

Wage growth that exceeds productivity for a sustained period may contribute to persistent services inflation.

# Cross Confirmation

MOS never evaluates inflation using one indicator alone.

Example:

Core CPI ↓

Core PCE ↓

PPI ↓

Breakeven Inflation Stable

↓

High confidence that inflation is moderating.

Example:

Core CPI ↓

Oil ↑

PPI ↑

Wages ↑

↓

Conflicting evidence.

Monitor future releases before changing the macro regime.

# Common Analytical Mistakes

Mistake 1

Focusing only on Headline CPI.

Correction:

Compare Headline, Core and PCE.

---

Mistake 2

Ignoring shelter's lagged effect.

Correction:

Use private rent data as supplementary evidence.

---

Mistake 3

Confusing one-month volatility with trend.

Correction:

Monitor three-month and six-month annualized inflation.

---

Mistake 4

Ignoring inflation expectations.

Correction:

Expectations often determine policy more than current inflation.

# Key Questions

Every inflation analysis should answer:

Is inflation accelerating or decelerating?

Which components are driving inflation?

Are inflation expectations anchored?

What does the inflation trend imply for Federal Reserve policy?

How should inflation affect asset allocation?

---

# Final Principle

Inflation is not a single statistic.

It is the interaction of demand, supply, labor costs, expectations and policy.

MOS seeks to identify the underlying inflation process rather than react to individual monthly releases.
