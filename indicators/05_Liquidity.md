# Liquidity Handbook

## Purpose

The Liquidity Handbook explains how MOS measures financial liquidity and evaluates its influence on economic activity and asset prices.

Liquidity represents the availability of money and credit within the financial system.

Changes in liquidity often affect financial markets before they appear in traditional macroeconomic indicators.

MOS therefore treats liquidity as one of the core drivers of market performance.

---

### Why It Matters

The Federal Reserve balance sheet reflects the amount of liquidity supplied to the financial system.

Balance sheet expansion generally increases reserves.

Balance sheet reduction withdraws liquidity.

MOS monitors both the level and the pace of change.

---

# Core Indicator 2

## Quantitative Easing (QE)

Importance

★★★★★

Classification

Policy Tool

---

QE increases central bank asset holdings.

It is designed to lower long-term interest rates and improve financial conditions.

QE generally supports liquidity-sensitive assets.

---

# Core Indicator 3

## Quantitative Tightening (QT)

Importance

★★★★★

Classification

Policy Tool

---

QT reduces central bank asset holdings.

It gradually removes liquidity from the financial system.

QT may tighten financial conditions even if policy rates remain unchanged.

---

# Core Indicator 4

## Reverse Repo Facility (RRP)

Importance

★★★★★

Classification

Liquidity Indicator

---

The Reverse Repo Facility measures the amount of cash parked at the Federal Reserve by eligible counterparties.

A declining RRP balance often indicates that liquidity is flowing back into the financial system.

MOS interprets changes in RRP together with reserve balances and Treasury issuance.

---

# Core Indicator 5

## Treasury General Account (TGA)

Importance

★★★★★

Classification

Liquidity Indicator

---

The Treasury General Account is the U.S. government's checking account at the Federal Reserve.

Changes in the TGA influence the amount of liquidity available to the private sector.

Rising TGA balances generally withdraw liquidity.

Falling balances generally inject liquidity.

---

# Core Indicator 6

## M2 Money Supply

Importance

★★★★☆

Publisher

Federal Reserve

Classification

Coincident

---

M2 measures broad money held by households and businesses.

Although its relationship with inflation varies over time, M2 remains a useful indicator of long-term monetary conditions.

MOS uses M2 as a supporting indicator rather than a standalone signal.

---

# Core Indicator 7

## Financial Conditions Index (FCI)

Importance

★★★★★

Classification

Composite

---

The Financial Conditions Index combines information from interest rates, credit spreads, exchange rates and equity markets.

It provides a broad assessment of whether financial conditions are becoming more restrictive or more accommodative.

MOS uses the FCI as a high-level confirmation of liquidity conditions.

# Common Analytical Mistakes

Mistake 1

Focusing only on policy rates.

Correction:

Liquidity is influenced by both interest rates and central bank balance sheet policies.

---

Mistake 2

Ignoring TGA and RRP.

Correction:

Treasury cash management can materially influence short-term liquidity.

---

Mistake 3

Assuming M2 alone explains market movements.

Correction:

Use M2 together with reserves, balance sheet changes and financial conditions.

# Key Questions

Every liquidity analysis should answer:

Is system liquidity expanding or contracting?

Which source is driving the change?

Are financial conditions easing or tightening?

How should liquidity conditions influence asset allocation?

---

# Final Principle

Liquidity is the fuel of the financial system.

Economic fundamentals determine long-term value.

Liquidity determines how quickly markets move toward or away from that value.
